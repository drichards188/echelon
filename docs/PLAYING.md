# How to play

Back to the [README](../README.md).

## The screen

The left half of the screen is a third-person view of the unit you have selected; the right half is that unit's console. The units you are not operating fight on their own, in whatever mode you left them.

## The title screen

A demonstration battle runs behind it. Its camera drifts slowly and cuts to another unit about every 26 s, and now and then to a missile launch. Pick an era, a difficulty and a loadout and click **Take command**.

- **Era.** The toggle at the top of the options picks **Modern** (2020s) or **WW2** (1944 to 1945). The line under it gives the era's setting (point at the other era to read its own). An era brings its own map, units, raids and consoles; the loadout picker, the tutorials and the demonstration battle follow it. The era is kept in the browser for the next visit.
- **Difficulty.** **Easy**, **Normal** or **Hard** change the size of the raids, the hit chances of your missiles and shells, the damage that gets through (less on Easy) and, in WW2, how well the gun crews and the Allied aircraft aim.
- **Loadout.** Under the difficulty the title screen lists the five slots (the number keys) with their options, the default first: two each in the modern era, and in WW2 so far only slot 5 has a choice. The line under a slot gives the chosen option's trade-off (point at the other option to read its own).
- **Trying a loadout.** Picking an option rebuilds the demonstration battle with it and cuts to that unit, so you see what you chose; **Take command** fights with it. The choice is kept in the browser for the next visit, one loadout per era.

## The battle

- **The first raid** comes about 15 s after Take command, called on the radio.
- **Between raids** (40 s of quiet) a supply column refills the reserves. Launchers still have to reload, which takes from half a minute to two minutes and keeps that launcher out of action, so start them as the raid ends.
- **In WW2** the guns' ammunition is brought up the same way, and the runway, if it still stands, gets back a quarter of its strength.
- **You win** when every raid has been beaten off.
- **You lose** when the runway and the command post (in WW2 the Flak command post) are both destroyed, or when no unit is left.
- **Score.** Every kill scores points; a weapon that gets through and hits something costs half its points.
- **The end screen** gives the score, kills, leakers, missiles spent and their cost (in WW2 the rounds fired), damage taken and units still standing.

## Modes

- **Auto.** Every unit starts in auto: its crew engages on its own, competently but wastefully (slow to react, it fires on decoys, uses the big missiles on everything and never switches its radar off).
- **Manual** fires only on your orders, and your missiles hit a little more often.
- **Hold** never fires.

Launching, firing, uncaging or slewing a sight puts a unit in auto into manual first.

## Modern era

In the 2020s you defend an airbase on the steppe. The default units are an S-400 battalion for the long-range, high layer, two Pantsir-S1 for point defense, and an Osa-AKM and a Strela-10M3 for the short-range layer.

**The raids.** Seven escalating raids of drones, cruise missiles, fighters with radar-seeking missiles and glide bombs, decoys, attack helicopters and ballistic missiles.

| Slot | Default | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 area layer | S-400 | Buk-M2 battery: 70 km, cheaper rounds and a deep reserve, one target per TELAR, no answer to ballistic missiles; five vehicles knocked out one by one |
| 2 battalion cover | Pantsir-S1 | 2S6M Tunguska: guns first (more 30 mm, its crew opens fire at 3.5 km, tougher), eight cheap 9M311 to 10 km guided only through the optical sight, a new missile target every 8 s at most |
| 3 airbase cover | Pantsir-S1 | 2S6M Tunguska |
| 4 short range | Osa-AKM | Tor-M2: four targets at once inside its radar sector (a new one every 4 s at most), quick, best against small fast targets, dearer rounds fired straight up out of their cells |
| 5 very short range | Strela-10M3 | ZSU-23-4M4 Shilka-M4: four 23 mm guns, free to fire, its shells reach about 2.8 km and its crew opens fire inside 2 km; its radar emits |

**The layers.** The search radars see high flyers far out and low flyers only close in and over open ground. So the long layer gets the aircraft, decoys and ballistic missiles, and what comes in low leaks through to the Pantsirs, the Osa and the Strela.

**S-400** (battalion command post): the big scope and the track table.

- **Launching.** Pick the 48N6 (long range, few) or the 9M96 (shorter range, cheap and plentiful), select a track and launch one or two. **NEXT THREAT** steps through the threats nobody is engaging yet.
- **Decoys.** Slow "aircraft" that never close are often decoys: their class shows once a track has been held long enough.
- **Going dark.** When a radar-seeking missile comes for the battalion, switching the emitter off makes it lose its aim, but blinds the battalion and its 48N6 in flight.
- **The data link.** While it stands (the Buk: while its command post stands) it fuses every emitting unit's tracks into one picture and links it to the others; without it they show NO LINK.

**Pantsir-S1:**

- **Missiles** out to 20 km on two channels, a new target every 6 s at most (about ten a minute; a re-fire at a target its missile missed goes at once).
- **Guns.** Twin 30 mm guns whose shells reach about 4.2 km, taking about 3 s to 2 km and 7 s to 4 km and scattering more the farther they go, so the crew opens fire inside 2.5 km. In manual, hold Space or the FIRE button: it greys out while the shells cannot reach the target.
- **Tracking.** The tracking channel can be the radar or the silent electro-optical sight, whose picture is in the console.

**Osa-AKM:** one target at a time, six missiles to 10 km fired singly or in pairs; its tracking radar must hold the target until the missiles arrive.

**Strela-10M3:** no radar.

- **Slew** the optical sight onto the target (drag in the sight or arrow keys).
- **Cue.** The strip below the sight cues the bearings of the nearest threats from the battalion's picture; clicking a cue lays the sight on it.
- **Fire.** Uncage the seeker (U), wait for the tone to steady and the LOCK lamp, then fire.

## WW2 era

You command the German flak at Hopsten, a jet airfield in north-west Germany, in February and March 1945: a battery of six 8.8 cm guns laid from its predictor, two 3.7 cm platoons, a platoon of 2 cm Flakvierlinge and a what-if concrete flak tower.

**The raids.** Six day raids of a Spitfire photo aircraft, Mustangs, Thunderbolts, rocket Typhoons, Tempests, Invaders and B-17 boxes dropping Window, the last on the day of the Rhine crossing.

| Slot | Default | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 heavy battery | 8.8 cm Flak 37 battery (Ludwig): six guns fired together from the Kommandogeraet 40 and the Wuerzburg radar, with two 2 cm guns of its own; with the flak tower's 12.8 cm, the only guns that reach the bombers at height | none yet |
| 2 medium, Flak lane | 3.7 cm Flak 43 platoon (Martha): three guns on their own sights, fed clip by clip, reach to about 3 km | none yet |
| 3 medium, dispersal | 3.7 cm Flak 43 platoon (Nordpol) | none yet |
| 4 light, quadruple | 2 cm Flakvierling 38 platoon (Otto): three quadruple mounts, one on a wooden tower, a wall of fire inside 1.5 km | none yet |
| 5 light | Flak tower (Paula; a what-if, the real ones stood in Berlin, Hamburg and Vienna): four twin 12.8 cm on its corner turrets laid from the predictor on its roof, two 3.7 cm on the roof and four Vierlinge on the galleries | 2 cm Flak 38 platoon: three single guns, short reach, quick to swing onto a diving attacker |

Alternatives for slots 1 to 4 come later (`DESIGN.md`, "WW2 era").

- **Units.** Every unit is a group of guns, each with its own crew.
- **Modes.** FREI (auto), BEFEHL (manual) and HALT (hold).
- **The plot.** Aircraft come onto the plot from the lookouts, the Wuerzburg and the aircraft reporting service. The reporting service calls by telephone through the Flak command post, so the plot is lost with it.

**8.8 cm battery:** the six guns share the Kommandogeraet 40.

- **Firing.** Select a plotted aircraft, let the solution settle, then hold **FEUER** (Space) for salvos, or click **EINZEL** to fire one ready gun.
- **Range and radar.** The console picks the range source (the rangefinder or the Wuerzburg, T; Window spoils the radar) and switches the Wuerzburg (R).
- **Barrage.** G lays a barrage box on a formation's course, which works without the predictor and through Window.

**3.7 cm and 2 cm platoons:** three guns with their own sights and ammunition feeds (clips on a loading table, magazines changed by the loaders).

- **Manual.** All three take the selected target and fire while FEUER is held.
- **Auto.** Each crew picks its own, and G puts them all on the selected one.
- **Sights.** Q switches the sight picture to the next gun (or click a gun's card).

**Flak tower:** one unit with three kinds of gun.

- **Gun selector.** GESCHÜTZWAHL (or W) picks the ones you command: the twin 12.8 cm on the predictor as a battery, or the 3.7 cm or the Vierlinge as a platoon.
- **The rest** fight on with their crews unless the mode is HALT.
- **Masking.** The tower's own walls mask its light guns, so a gun that would fire through the concrete holds (VERDECKT).

## Tutorials

**Tutorials** on the title screen opens a list of short guided lessons for the era in play: one for each vehicle or gun unit, the loadout alternatives included, and in the modern era one for the basics. A tick shows the ones you have finished (kept in the browser's local storage).

- **A quiet range.** Each lesson runs with the raids off: nothing is scored, nothing can be destroyed, and the other units stand down.
- **Steps.** A card over the 3D view gives the step, a pulsing amber frame marks the console control it is about, and the step completes when you do the thing.
- **Next and Skip.** Steps that only explain go on with **Next**; **Skip step** passes one you cannot complete.
- **Time compression** stays in your hands: the + and - buttons by the time readout, or [ and ].
- **Leaving.** Esc or **Exit** goes back to the list, Esc again to the title.
- **Your loadout.** A lesson runs on the default units with its own vehicle swapped in (the Buk, Tunguska, Tor and Shilka lessons, and the WW2 2 cm Flak 38 lesson) and puts your loadout back when it ends.

### The lessons

- **The basics**: orbit and zoom, switching units (1 to 5, the tabs), modes, padlock (V), the missile chase (F), time compression, the layers.
- **S-400 battalion**: the scope and the track table, manual mode, the long and the short missile, launching at an aircraft, a decoy that gives itself away (hold fire), switching the emitter off for an anti-radar missile, reloading.
- **Pantsir-S1**: the search scope and the tracking channel, missiles and then guns, a drone close in, the silent TV / IR channel.
- **Osa-AKM**: the scope, the tracking radar, a salvo of two, the one-target limit, reloading.
- **Strela-10M3**: the bearing cue strip, slewing the sight, uncaging, the tone and the lock lamp, firing, the stowed reload.
- **Buk-M2 battery** (replaces the S-400): the five vehicles and why each TELAR is its own fire unit, the scope and track table, naming the firing vehicle, the TELAR radar holding the target to impact, two targets on two TELARs, going dark against an anti-radar missile, reloading a TELAR from the loader-launcher.
- **2S6M Tunguska** (replaces a Pantsir): holding the trigger and the fire control's lead, heat and ammunition, the optical-only missile channel and when to use it, the search radar, reloading.
- **Tor-M2** (replaces the Osa): the tracking sector, a vertical cold launch, several targets at once on separate channels, a salvo, reloading.
- **Shilka-M4** (replaces the Strela): radar tracking and the lock lamp, the lead mark in the sight, a very short range, bursts and heat, the optical fallback with the dish off, the radar as an anti-radar target.
- **WW2 8.8 cm battery**: the air picture, designating a target, letting the solution settle, salvos, reading the bursts, optical ranging when Window spoils the radar, and barrage fire.
- **WW2 flak tower**: the gun selector, twin 12.8 cm salvos on a bomber box, then the Vierlinge and the 3.7 cm in hand against a Mustang strafing the tower while the twins fight on with their crews.
- **WW2 3.7 cm, Flakvierling and 2 cm platoons**: one lesson per gun type, covering the sight, the reach, the ammunition feed, naming the target, firing, and spreading or concentrating the fire (the 2 cm lesson swaps the Flak 38 platoon in for the tower).

### Starting a lesson from the address bar

- **Modern:** `?tutorial=s400` (or `basics`, `pantsir`, `osa`, `strela`, `buk`, `tunguska`, `tor`, `shilka`) starts a modern lesson at once.
- **WW2:** with `?era=ww2`, `?tutorial=flak88` (or `flakturm`, `flak43`, `flakvierling`, `flak38`) starts a WW2 one.
